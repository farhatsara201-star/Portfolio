01 · Context

Fragmented intake for customers; a path beyond fax for Concord

The customer problem: fragmented document intake

Healthcare teams receive documents through different portals, fax, and other points of intake. Staff have to monitor those destinations, consolidate the documents, and reconcile them before routing the information into the right workflow. Adding another standalone inbox would compound that fragmentation.

The business problem: expanding beyond fax into IDP

Concord wanted to grow beyond its cloud-fax business into intelligent document processing. Fax provided unstructured document intake; the broader strategy required a way to receive structured clinical documents as well. Direct Secure Messaging supplied a missing interoperability capability, bringing structured C-CDA documents into Concord Connect alongside PDFs and images.

DSM therefore served both the customer workflow and the business strategy: it added another exchange channel to a consolidated intake experience and helped position Concord for a business beyond fax. The channel alone did not perform intelligent document processing; it supplied the intake infrastructure that the broader IDP strategy depended on.

The product question How could we add structured clinical document exchange to Concord Connect, support the expansion into IDP, and consolidate customer intake without creating another destination for staff to monitor?

03 · Key decisions

Scope the first release around reliable intake

The long-term opportunity was a unified, multimodal intake layer that could receive structured and unstructured documents, classify them, and route them into downstream workflows. The first release still needed a boundary that the team could implement and validate without pretending the entire vision was already solved.

MVP boundary Phase 1 focused on inbound DSM and the document types customers already needed to manage—structured C-CDA files alongside PDFs and images. Broader automation remained part of the architecture, but not a dependency for the initial release.

Vendor selection

The HISP choice affected certification, delivery reliability, implementation effort, contracting, support, and the product’s future flexibility. I compared four vendors across technical and commercial criteria, worked with engineering on validation spikes, and selected Kno2. The integration was designed to reduce unnecessary vendor lock-in.

Workflow design

DSM was designed as part of Concord’s broader document-intake experience rather than a separate product surface. That decision kept the work anchored in how customers triage documents and reduced the risk of adding another isolated queue.

04 · Delivery

Product delivery included the operating system around the feature

The implementation work extended beyond the protocol integration. The team needed shared product language, release sequencing, support documentation, and clarity about which healthcare settings and workflows should shape early commercial conversations.

I mapped seven care settings—including FQHCs, skilled nursing facilities, long-term acute-care hospitals, revenue-cycle organizations, and specialty clinics—to users, jobs to be done, and relevant document workflows. I also created the user manual, maintained a living internal question-and-answer resource, and delivered six education sessions across executive, commercial, implementation, and support teams.

05 · Outcomes

Structured intake launched; a foundation for growth beyond fax

Direct Secure Messaging progressed from market assessment to private beta and a public debut at HIMSS 2026. The work established structured clinical document intake alongside existing fax, PDF, and image workflows, supporting Concord’s expansion into IDP. It also established the internal commercial and support infrastructure required to bring the capability to customers.
