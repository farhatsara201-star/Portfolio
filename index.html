Provider performance modeling

Extending the model
to general surgery.

Problem

Healthcare organizations and ACOs used provider scores to assess existing networks and identify physicians to add. General surgery was a gap in the product’s specialty coverage.

My contribution

I adapted an existing Python modeling pipeline: identified providers through public information and claims, cleaned the data, engineered patient-risk features, and evaluated model outputs. The data science lead approved release, and the scores became available to customers.

OutcomeGeneral-surgery performance scores approved by the data science lead and released to customers.

Explore my contribution

I extended an existing provider-performance modeling pipeline to general surgery, adapting the analysis to a specialty with substantial variation in procedures, episodes of care, and patient complexity. The challenge was to produce meaningful risk-adjusted comparisons across a heterogeneous surgical population.

My work combined provider taxonomy validation, claims-based analysis, data preparation, feature engineering, and iterative evaluation of model fit. Public provider information and billed procedures helped establish clinically appropriate cohorts. The analysis incorporated a broad set of patient, episode-of-care, and cost measures, including HCC risk scores, social determinants, demographics, episode characteristics, and expenditure variables. Per-member-per-year (PMPY) costs formed part of the wider analytical context alongside episode-level cost and efficiency measures.

I examined observed-versus-predicted results and statistical fit, investigating low explanatory power and overfitting or underfitting as I refined the general-surgery model. I worked in Python and AWS, with the data science lead providing release approval. The resulting scores became available to customers for provider-performance assessment and network expansion.

About

A systems view of healthcare products

I began as a clinical dietitian, working across inpatient and outpatient care. I later moved into acute-care transitions and prior authorization, where product problems showed up as missed handoffs, delayed access, and work that depended on people compensating for fragmented systems.

At CareJourney, I moved closer to the data: longitudinal Medicare and Medicaid claims, provider-performance modeling, attribution, and value-based-care reporting. At Concord, I moved into product management and applied that clinical and analytical background to B2B healthcare software.

That path shapes how I work now. I look at the feature, but I also look at the workflow around it, the data it depends on, the incentives behind it, and what has to be true for people to use it reliably.
