2. MDS 3.0 — Every Section

What it is: A federally mandated standardized assessment tool. Every Medicare/Medicaid SNF resident must be assessed using it. It drives care planning, quality reporting, AND Medicare reimbursement (PDPM).

Who fills it: The MDS Coordinator (usually an RN) pulls data from the entire interdisciplinary team — nursing, therapy, social work, dietary, pharmacy.

Key fact: The Day 5 assessment is the most financially critical. It locks in the PDPM payment rate for the entire stay. If diagnoses are under-coded on Day 5, the SNF loses money for every subsequent day. This is why PDPM Coach exists.

4. PDPM — How Medicare Pays the SNF

Before PDPM (pre-2019): SNFs were paid based on therapy minutes. More minutes = more money. Led to unnecessary therapy.

After PDPM (2019–present): Payment based on the patient's clinical characteristics from the MDS.

PDPM Daily Rate = PT + OT + SLP + Nursing + NTA

6. Physician Workflow — Product Strategy Insight

The Sign-In Fix (Surface Level)

  • SSO — integrate with the hospital's existing identity provider. Physician never has a separate PCC password.
  • Magic links via SMS — tap a link, you're in. No password to remember.
  • Biometric login on mobile — Face ID / fingerprint. Zero friction.

Any of these eliminates the reset loop. But this is the wrong framing.

Why Does PCC Keep Forcing Password Resets?

  1. Outdated security policy — Healthcare IT has historically required resets every 30–90 days. NIST reversed this in 2017 — forced resets make security worse because users write passwords down. Healthcare IT is slow to update.
  2. Infrequent use triggers lockout — Visiting physician rounds once or twice a week. Many systems lock accounts after X days of inactivity. Weekly rounds + 30-day policy = locked out almost every visit.
  3. External user treatment — SNF physicians aren't employed by the facility. External accounts get shorter sessions, faster expiration, no SSO with home hospital credentials.
  4. No SSO — PCC credentials are completely separate. They expire while the physician isn't paying attention.
"The reset policy exists to satisfy a compliance requirement. The outcome is a folder of plaintext passwords in a laptop bag. That's the gap between compliance theater and actual security — and it's a product design problem PCC owns."

The Power User Signal

A physician friend who actually uses PCC carries his laptop to SNFs and keeps a physical folder of passwords. This matters more than the physician who gave up — because it shows the problem isn't resistance to technology. It's authentication UX so broken that even committed users route around it with paper.

When the workaround is a folder, the fix isn't training. It's SSO.

The Real Insight: Don't Fight the Paper — Make It Intelligent

Most SNF physicians are older and paper-preferring. Even with perfect SSO, a physician who has written notes by hand for 30 years isn't switching to a portal. The behavior is structural.

"My husband gave up on PCC entirely — password resets every time. His friend carries a laptop to SNFs and keeps a physical folder of passwords. When your most committed user's solution is a paper folder, you don't have an adoption problem. You have an authentication problem. But even fixing SSO doesn't solve the underlying issue — most SNF physicians are paper-preferring regardless of login friction. The durable product answer is IDP on scanned notes: make the paper intelligent rather than trying to eliminate it."

7. PCC Product Suite — Likely Portfolio for This Role

Important: The JD says "portfolio of Senior Care products" — you're owning multiple products. Products explicitly named in the JD are marked ★.

Smart question to ask: "Can you tell me which products are in scope for this role? I want to make sure I'm thinking about the right areas going in."

★ JD MDS / PDPM Coach

What it is: The tool MDS coordinators use to complete the Day 5 Medicare assessment. Shows them in real time how their coding decisions affect Medicare reimbursement. Flags diagnoses that are under-documented before submission to CMS.

Why it matters: Day 5 locks in the payment rate for the entire stay. PCC's most financially critical SNF product.

Current gap: Guidance-based only — helps coordinators code better, doesn't automate intake. No AI extraction from physician notes. The scanned notes → undercoding problem is still unaddressed.

★ JD LTCF / interRAI

What it is: Canada's replacement for MDS. PCC is actively migrating Canadian customers to interRAI LTCF right now.

Why it matters: PCC is a Canadian company (HQ: Mississauga). Major in-progress product initiative.

Plain English: MDS but for Canada — different sections, different regulatory requirements, full platform migration underway.

★ JD Immunizations

What it is: Tracks resident immunization records — flu shots, COVID boosters, pneumococcal vaccines. Manages schedules, consent, and CMS reporting.

Why it matters: CMS requires SNFs to report immunization rates for quality star ratings. Missing documentation = lower star rating = lower occupancy.

★ JD Clinical Documentation

What it is: Core nursing workflow — assessments, care plans, progress notes, point-of-care charting on tablets/mobile.

Why it matters: Everything else feeds from it. If nursing documentation is incomplete, MDS, care plans, and downstream products break.

Nutrition Management

What it is: Tracks dietary assessments, meal preferences, weight, and nutritional interventions.

Sara's connection: Feeds directly into MDS Section K — the section Sara owned as a dietitian. Weight loss flags, therapeutic diet orders, and swallowing interventions documented here affect SLP reimbursement under PDPM.

Medication Management

What it is: Electronic medication administration record (eMAR). Nurses document every medication given, flag missed doses, reconcile pharmacy orders.

Why it matters: CMS tracks antipsychotic use as a quality indicator — documented in MDS Section N.

Skin & Wound

What it is: Tracks pressure injuries and wounds — location, stage, dimensions, photos, treatment history.

Why it matters: Major CMS quality measure and litigation risk. Feeds into MDS Section M.

Practitioner Engagement

What it is: The physician/NP portal for orders, notes, and ePrescribing.

Why it matters: Known adoption failure. See Section 5 & 6 of this guide.

Care Insights

What it is: Analytics dashboard — population health views, early warning flags, census reporting, quality metrics.

Why it matters: Gives administrators and Directors of Nursing visibility across the resident population without digging into individual charts.
